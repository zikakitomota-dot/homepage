What Is TDEE?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It is an estimate of all the energy your body uses in a day, including energy for essential body functions, everyday movement, and planned exercise.

Many people use TDEE as a starting estimate of their maintenance calories — roughly the daily calorie intake that may maintain their current weight.

How Is TDEE Calculated?

This calculator first estimates your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) with the Mifflin–St Jeor equation. It then multiplies that result by the activity factor that matches your selection.

TDEE = BMR × activity factor

The activity factors used are sedentary 1.2, lightly active 1.375, moderately active 1.55, very active 1.725, and extra active 1.9. This five-step ladder is a convenient estimation convention, not an individually measured physical-activity level and not part of the original Mifflin–St Jeor paper. Daily movement, work, exercise, and variation between days make the selected level an important source of uncertainty.

Example: A person with a BMR of 1,700 kcal/day selects moderately active, which uses a factor of 1.55.

1,700 × 1.55 = 2,635 kcal/day

Their estimated TDEE, and therefore their starting estimate for maintenance calories, is 2,635 kcal/day.

BMR vs TDEE: What Is the Difference?

BMR estimates the calories your body needs at rest to support essential functions such as breathing and circulation. TDEE includes that resting energy plus an allowance for daily activity, so it is the more useful estimate when considering total calorie needs.

BMR and TDEE calculations use related numbers, but they answer different questions: BMR estimates resting needs, while TDEE estimates full-day energy expenditure.

How to Use Your Maintenance Calorie Estimate

Use your result as a starting reference rather than an exact target. It can help you understand your approximate maintenance intake or provide context for a gradual weight goal. If you are planning weight loss, the Calorie Deficit Calculator can turn this estimate into a conservative goal-based starting point.

TDEE can vary with body composition, health, routine, and day-to-day movement. Comparing your estimated intake with your weight trend over several weeks can provide more useful context than relying on a single calculated number.

Other Useful Health Estimates

TDEE describes energy use, not body size or a recommended weight. For separate context, use the BMI Calculator for an adult height-and-weight screening estimate or the Reference Weight Calculator to compare historical height-based formulas.

Use the Activity Multiplier as a Starting Hypothesis

The activity labels compress work, exercise, and everyday movement into one multiplier. A job with long periods of standing and three workouts a week may not match a neat label, so the first result is best treated as a hypothesis to check.

  1. Choose the description closest to a typical week, not your busiest day.
  2. Keep the estimate separate from what a tracker reports as “calories burned.”
  3. If you use the number for planning, compare it with a multi-week pattern rather than one day.
  4. Recalculate when weight or routine changes materially.

Agreement with a short-term scale change does not prove the multiplier is exact; water balance and intake estimates also vary.

Frequently asked questions

What does TDEE mean?

TDEE stands for Total Daily Energy Expenditure. It estimates the total calories your body uses in a day through basic functions, daily movement, and exercise.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR estimates the energy your body needs at rest for essential functions. TDEE starts with BMR and applies an activity factor to estimate your total daily calorie needs.

Is TDEE the same as maintenance calories?

TDEE is commonly used as an estimate of maintenance calories: the daily intake that may help you maintain your current weight. Your actual maintenance intake can differ from the estimate.

How should I choose an activity level?

Choose the option that best reflects both your usual daily movement and planned exercise across a typical week. A realistic selection produces a more useful estimate than choosing based on your most active day.

Why is a TDEE calculation only an estimate?

The equation cannot fully account for differences in body composition, metabolism, health, or day-to-day movement. Use the result as a starting reference and consider your real-world weight trend over time.

When is a TDEE calculator useful?

A TDEE calculator can provide a starting point for understanding daily calorie needs, estimating maintenance calories, or planning a gradual weight goal. It is not a personalised nutrition prescription.
