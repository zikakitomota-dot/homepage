How it works

Maintenance calories are the estimated calories needed to maintain your current weight. This calculator estimates them as Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE), combining estimated resting energy needs with your selected activity level. Choosing a realistic activity level makes the estimate more useful, but real daily expenditure still varies.

It then converts your selected pace into a daily deficit and subtracts it from maintenance:

Daily deficit = (target kg per week × 7,700) ÷ 7

Calorie target = TDEE − daily deficit

The calculator uses the static 7,700 kcal/kg conversion only to turn the selected pace into arithmetic: the 0.5 kg/week option becomes about 550 kcal per day. Research on body-weight dynamics shows that actual change does not follow a fixed conversion indefinitely. The pace options are planning estimates, not recommendations, and the largest option is not automatically appropriate. A safety note appears if the calculated target falls below the tool's displayed floor.

For a maintenance-focused estimate and more detail about activity multipliers, use the TDEE Calculator.

Example calculation

Person: someone with a TDEE of 2,400 kcal aiming to lose 0.5 kg per week.

Daily deficit = (0.5 × 7,700) ÷ 7 ≈ 550 kcal

Target = 2,400 − 550 = 1,850 kcal/day

1,850 calories per day is the resulting planning estimate. Actual weight change may differ because energy expenditure, food tracking, water weight, and normal fluctuations vary.

A Static Scenario, Not a Diet Recommendation

This result combines two estimates: TDEE depends on an equation and activity multiplier, while the selected pace uses a fixed energy conversion. Their uncertainty compounds, and the relationship between intake and body weight changes over time.

The displayed floor is a warning rule inside this tool, not proof that a value above it is suitable. This calculator is not designed for pregnancy, breastfeeding, growth, illness, eating-disorder recovery, or circumstances involving clinical treatment. Personal planning in those situations belongs with a qualified healthcare professional.

Frequently asked questions

What is a calorie deficit?

A calorie deficit means eating fewer calories than your body burns in a day. Over time this prompts the body to use stored energy, which can lead to weight loss.

What should my calorie deficit be?

There is no single suitable deficit for everyone. It depends on your estimated maintenance calories, body size, activity, and individual circumstances. This calculator converts each pace option into an estimated daily deficit; a larger deficit is not automatically better or more appropriate.

What are maintenance calories?

Maintenance calories are the estimated calories needed to maintain your current weight. Here they are estimated from your BMR and selected activity level, producing an estimated Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE).

How does TDEE relate to a calorie deficit?

TDEE is used as the maintenance estimate. The calculator subtracts the deficit associated with your selected pace from that estimate to produce a daily calorie target.

Why 7,700 calories per kilogram?

This calculator uses 7,700 kcal per kilogram as a simple conversion rule. Human weight change is dynamic and includes changing proportions of fat, lean tissue, glycogen, and water, so the conversion cannot predict an exact weekly loss.

Does a calorie deficit guarantee weight loss?

No. The result is a planning estimate, not a guarantee. Real energy expenditure, food tracking, water weight, and normal scale fluctuations can all make actual results differ.

Is a larger calorie deficit always better?

No. Larger deficits can produce very low targets and may be harder to sustain. Choose a realistic pace and seek personalised advice from a qualified healthcare professional when needed.

Is a calorie deficit safe for everyone?

Not always. Needs vary, and very low intake targets may not be appropriate. This tool is educational only — talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before starting a weight-loss plan, especially if you have health conditions or need personalised dietary advice.
