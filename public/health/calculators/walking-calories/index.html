Walking Calories Calculator

Estimate how many calories you burn on a walk based on your weight, walking pace, and how long you walk.

Your body weight helps personalize the energy estimate.
Speed selects the walking intensity and calculates distance.

Enter any duration, such as 30, 40, 45, or 60 minutes.
Distance is calculated automatically from walking speed × duration; there is no separate distance or incline input.

Estimated calories burned

—

    This is an estimate based on body weight, walking intensity, and duration. Terrain, incline, fitness, and walking efficiency can change actual calorie burn.

    How it works

    The calculator uses MET values (Metabolic Equivalent of Task) to represent the energy cost of different walking speeds:

    Calories = MET × weight (kg) × time (hours)

    The current model groups speed into simplified bands: 2.0 MET below 3.2 km/h, 2.8 from 3.2 to under 4.0 km/h, 3.5 from 4.0 to under 4.8 km/h, 4.3 from 4.8 to under 5.6 km/h, 5.0 from 5.6 to under 6.4 km/h, and 6.3 at 6.4 km/h or faster. The Adult Compendium contains more specific walking activities than these six bands; terrain, incline, load, surface, and individual efficiency are not captured here. Imperial inputs are converted before the formula runs.

    Distance and duration

    Distance is estimated as walking speed × time. To estimate a 2 km, 4 km, or 5 km walk, enter your expected speed and adjust the duration until the calculated distance matches your route. For example, 5 km at 5 km/h takes 60 minutes.

    The result also calculates kcal/hour = total calories ÷ hours and, when distance is positive, kcal/km = total calories ÷ kilometres. These rates describe the entered walk and are not universal constants.

    Level walking and incline

    This calculator currently models level walking. Incline, hills, rough terrain, wind, individual fitness, and walking efficiency can change actual energy use, so the result should be treated as an estimate.

    Walking calories and weight change

    Calories burned while walking contribute to daily energy expenditure, but they do not guarantee weight loss. Body-weight change depends on overall energy intake and expenditure over time. Use the TDEE Calculator to understand daily energy needs or the Calorie Deficit Calculator to plan a sustainable target.

    Example calculation

    Person: 70 kg, walking at 5 km/h (MET 4.3) for 60 minutes.

    Calories = 4.3 × 70 × 1 = 301 kcal

    The calculator estimates 301 kcal, a distance of 5 km, a rate of 301 kcal/hour, and about 60 kcal/km.

    Methodology and sources

    Frequently asked questions

    How many calories do I burn walking?

    Enter your body weight, walking speed, and duration. The calculator selects a MET value for that speed and estimates calories with MET × weight in kilograms × time in hours.

    How many calories does a 5 km walk burn?

    There is no universal total. It depends on body weight, walking speed, and conditions. This calculator estimates distance from your speed and duration, so adjust the duration until the result shows about 5 km.

    How many calories do I burn walking for an hour?

    Set the duration to 60 minutes and enter your weight and walking speed. The result shows total calories and the estimated calorie burn per hour.

    Does walking speed affect calories burned?

    Yes. The calculator assigns higher MET values at faster walking speeds, increasing the estimated calories burned per minute and per hour.

    Does body weight affect walking calorie burn?

    Yes. For the same speed and duration, moving a larger body mass generally requires more energy, so the estimate rises with body weight.

    Does incline increase calories burned?

    Walking uphill generally requires more energy, but this calculator currently estimates level walking and does not directly adjust for treadmill incline, hills, or terrain.

    How are calories burned per kilometre calculated?

    When distance is available, the calculator divides total estimated calories by the calculated distance. This rate is specific to the entered weight and speed and should not be treated as universal.

    Are walking calorie calculators accurate?

    They provide estimates, not exact measurements. Fitness, walking efficiency, terrain, incline, wind, and differences between wearable devices can all affect actual energy use.
