What Is Basal Metabolic Rate?

BMR stands for Basal Metabolic Rate. It represents the energy your body is estimated to use while completely at rest to keep essential processes running, including breathing, circulation, cell activity, and temperature regulation.

A BMR calculator estimates this resting energy in calories per day. It does not include the additional energy used through standing, walking, work, exercise, or other daily activity.

How Is BMR Calculated?

This calculator uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation. The calculation combines weight, height, and age, then applies the constant for the selected sex:

Male: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age + 5

Female: BMR = 10 × weight(kg) + 6.25 × height(cm) − 5 × age − 161

The male and female selections use different final constants. Imperial inputs are converted to kilograms and centimetres before the same equation is applied.

Example: A 30-year-old woman is 165 cm tall and weighs 62 kg.

BMR = 10 × 62 + 6.25 × 165 − 5 × 30 − 161

= 1,340 kcal/day

Her estimated resting energy use is 1,340 kcal/day. This is a BMR estimate, not her total daily calorie requirement.

BMR vs Resting Metabolic Rate

Basal Metabolic Rate and resting metabolic rate (RMR) both describe energy used while resting, so the terms are often used similarly in everyday calorie-planning contexts. Technically, BMR is measured under stricter conditions, including complete rest and fasting, while an RMR measurement may use less restrictive conditions.

This page provides an equation-based BMR estimate; it does not clinically measure either BMR or RMR.

BMR vs TDEE: Resting Energy and Daily Energy

BMR = estimated energy used at rest

TDEE = estimated total daily energy use after activity is included

You should not normally treat BMR alone as an everyday calorie target because normal daily activity requires additional energy. Use the TDEE Calculator to add an activity factor and estimate total daily energy needs.

What Affects BMR?

The equation uses age, selected sex, height, and weight because these variables help predict resting energy expenditure. Actual metabolism can also vary with body composition, genetics, health, and measurement conditions, which is why the result should be treated as an estimate.

For a mathematical weight-loss scenario based on total daily energy rather than BMR alone, use the Calorie Deficit Calculator.

Prediction Is Not the Same as Measurement

The equation predicts resting energy use from population data. It does not measure your metabolism in a laboratory, and two people with the same inputs can still have different measured results.

  • Changing weight or height changes the estimate because both are inputs to the equation.
  • Age reduces the estimate mathematically; it does not describe every age-related change in an individual.
  • Body composition, health, medication, and measurement conditions are not directly represented.

Use BMR to understand the scale of resting energy and as an input to a TDEE estimate. Do not read it as a measured metabolic rate, a minimum intake, or a personal prescription.

Frequently asked questions

What does BMR mean?

BMR stands for Basal Metabolic Rate. It estimates the energy your body uses at complete rest to support essential functions such as breathing, circulation, and maintaining body temperature.

How do you calculate BMR?

This calculator uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation. It combines your age, selected sex, height, and weight to estimate resting energy expenditure in calories per day.

Is BMR the same as resting metabolic rate?

The terms are often used similarly in everyday calorie planning. Technically, BMR is measured under stricter resting and fasting conditions, while resting metabolic rate can be measured under less restrictive conditions.

What is the difference between BMR and TDEE?

BMR estimates energy used at rest. TDEE adds an activity factor to estimate total daily energy use, including everyday movement and exercise. BMR alone is not a recommended everyday calorie target.

Does the BMR calculation differ for men and women?

The Mifflin–St Jeor equation uses a different final constant for the male and female selections. Choose the option that applies to you and the calculator adjusts the formula automatically.

What affects a BMR estimate?

The equation uses age, sex, height, and weight. Actual resting energy use can also vary with body composition, health, genetics, and measurement conditions.

Why is BMR only an estimate?

BMR equations predict resting energy use from population data rather than measuring it directly. Individual metabolism and body composition can cause actual values to differ.
